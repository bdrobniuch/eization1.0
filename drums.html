Drums

Put one groove or one rudiment on each line. In Edit, set Bars to two, and set the meter to the meter of that line. Play starts the click. When the bars finish, the next example comes up. Each example comes up once.

Hands stem up. The bass drum and the foot hat stem down. The book stays the book. These exercises are the lines. How to write drums shows the kit codes.

Backbeat

Eighth-note hats, snare on 2 and 4, and the kick moving under them. Open Backbeat.

Hat openings

Closed and open hats on the offbeats, one choke, and the foot hat on 2 and 4. Open Hat openings.

Jazz ride

The ride pattern, snare on 2 and 4, foot hat on 2 and 4. Two examples use the bell. Open Jazz ride.

Jazz waltz

The ride idea in 3/4. Open Jazz waltz.

Ghost notes

Quiet snare notes between the backbeats, hats steady, kick sparse. Open Ghost notes.

Rudiments

Singles, doubles, paradiddles, flams, and drags on the snare. No feet. Open Rudiments.

Linear fills

Sixteenth notes around the kit, one sound at a time, ending on a crash and a kick together. Open Linear fills.

Six eight

Hat eighths, the kick on the dotted quarter, snare on the fourth eighth. Open Six eight.

Twelve eight

That six-eight groove twice in the bar. Open Twelve eight.

Five four

Short ostinatos in 5/4. Open Five four.

Seven eight

Short ostinatos in 7/8. Open Seven eight.

Cross-stick and rim

Cross-stick comping with the hats running, then a rim shot on 2 and 4. Open Cross-stick and rim.

Chart marks

A repeating groove with a section name, a second bar that repeats, and one example at 96. Open Chart marks.

George Lawrence Stone, Stick Control

One sticking per line. The singles, doubles, and paradiddles are in Rudiments.

Joe Morello, Master Studies

One control pattern per line. Stay on the snare for Rudiments.

Ted Reed, Progressive Steps to Syncopation

One written rhythm per line. Chart marks puts a section name on a groove and repeats the bar. The same book is useful off the kit: it is named from Time.

John Riley, The Art of Bop Drumming

One ride, waltz, cross-stick, or chart mark per line. Open Jazz ride, Jazz waltz, Cross-stick and rim, or Chart marks.

John Riley, Beyond Bop Drumming

One waltz or odd-meter ostinato per line. Open Jazz waltz, Five four, or Seven eight.

Jim Chapin, Advanced Techniques for the Modern Drummer

One ride or foot-hat pattern per line. Open Jazz ride or Hat openings.

Gary Chester, The New Breed

One linear fill per line. Open Linear fills.

Gary Chaffee, Patterns

One sticking, one odd meter, or one time pattern per line. The four volumes stay in the books. Open Rudiments, Linear fills, or Seven eight.

David Garibaldi, Future Sounds

One ghost-note groove or one linear phrase per line. Open Ghost notes or Linear fills.

Rick Latham, Advanced Funk Studies

One backbeat or one ghost-note bar per line. Open Backbeat or Ghost notes.

Tommy Igoe, Groove Essentials

One backbeat, 6/8, or 12/8 groove per line. Open Backbeat, Six eight, or Twelve eight.

Ed Uribe, The Essence of Afro-Cuban Percussion and Drum Set

One 6/8 or 12/8 groove per line. Open Six eight or Twelve eight.

John Ramsay, The Drummer's Complete Vocabulary as Taught by Alan Dawson

One rudiment or one 6/8 ostinato per line. Open Rudiments or Six eight.

Kim Plainfield, Advanced Concepts

One 5/4 or 7/8 ostinato per line. Open Five four or Seven eight.
