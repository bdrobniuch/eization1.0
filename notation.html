How to write on the staff

Some exercises are large text. A line that parses as music is a staff instead. One example per line in Edit. The text below is the line. The staff under it is what you see.

The time signature is not in the line. It follows Setup. These figures use 4/4 unless the figure says otherwise. A line that does not parse stays text.

Try a line

Type one example. The staff updates as you write. Meter here is only for this box; in an exercise it follows Setup. Staff or Drums chooses how this box reads the line. Tap any sample on this page to load it here.

One pitch, then the durations

Letter, octave. Each note is its own word: C4 E4 is two notes; C4E4 is not. C4 is middle C, a quarter note. After the octave: w whole, h half, q quarter, e eighth, s sixteenth, n a filled notehead with no stem (one beat, C4n). A dot follows the duration: q. w.. Two dots, C4q.., make the note one and three quarters of the written value. n. and n.. do not parse. Accidentals: C#4, Db4, Cn4, Cx4 or C##4, Ebb4. A sign prints when that note differs from the key or from an earlier note in the bar. The next bar starts fresh. ! forces the sign.

Symbols in Edit stays closed until you open it. On a staff exercise it is a letter pad: tap a letter to write the note, then duration, a dot, the octave, accidentals, slash or rest, beam, tuplet, stack, undo, and erase. On a text exercise it inserts ♯ ♭ ♮ and the other chart marks. Typed marks parse the same way.

A MIDI keyboard does the same while Write notes is open, in Chrome or Edge on a computer. One key writes that pitch at the length that's lit. Keys held together write one stacked chord. The key of the line spells sharps or flats. Velocity, the pedal, and the clock stay unused.

Clef and key

@treble or @bass, and @key=, in either order. The default is treble, key of C. @key=Eb is E-flat major. @key=Am prints the signature of the relative major (C). A ! after the octave prints the accidental even when the key already has it (F#4!).

After a bar, @treble or @bass changes the clef for the rest of that example. The new clef is smaller. The key signature prints again after it. A clef change that is not after a bar is rejected.

In Edit, Meter (⋯) sets clef, key, and recommended meter for the whole exercise. Line @bass / @treble / @key= still override. The time signature is not typed in the line; a recommended meter can update Setup when you open the exercise. Staff beside Bars opens a short writing guide; tap a sample to insert that line.

Clef and key alone still draw an empty staff. A chord name with no note after it is a line chord on that empty staff.

A scale

^Major is any one word above the staff, before the notes — a scale name or a chord symbol. _1 is the degree under that note. _b3 prints as ♭3. Braces beam eighths and sixteenths into one group.

A chord

Brackets stack a chord. The length is written once after the closing bracket, so [E3 G3 B3 D4]h is a half and a missing length is a quarter. A length still written on a pitch is read, and the lowest note wins when they differ; every notehead uses that one length. ^ after the length is the chord name above the stack. _ is text under it. A chord can carry both. The name can use ∆. ^bVII and _bVII both print as ♭VII. _bbVII prints as 𝄫VII. Inside a chord word above the staff, _ is a space: ^Any_dom_7. A second keeps the note on the stem side in place and sets the next note to the right.

Rhythm

A slash has no pitch: /q, /e, /q.. Rests are r, rq, rh, rw, re, rs. A rest can take a dot: re. rq. rw.. | is a barline. A caption after · (space, middle dot, space) sits under the staff. _R is text under a slash or a rest. ^P is text above it. The same pair works on a note.

Beams

In 4/4, eighths and sixteenths beam with the beat even without braces, so eighths beam in pairs. Braces still force one group. In 6/8, eighths without braces still group in threes, because the beat is a dotted quarter. Set that meter in Setup. 4/4 draws as common time; 2/2 draws as cut time. The signature still comes from Setup, not from the line. A line that fills its bars draws a barline at the end. This last figure is drawn in 6/8.

Tuplets

Parentheses make a tuplet. (3 opens it and ) closes it, on the last note or on its own. (3 C4e D4e E4e) is three eighths in the time of two. With only the number, 3 is in the time of 2; 5, 6, and 7 in the time of 4; 9 in the time of 8; 2 and 4 in the time of 3. (5:4 sets the ratio and prints it.

Notes, slashes, rests, chords, and braces go inside, with any mix of durations. Eighths and sixteenths inside are beamed together. A group under one unbroken beam shows only the number. Quarters, halves, wholes, a rest, or a broken beam get a bracket. One note inside the parentheses still parses, and the staff draws that note plain. A tuplet does not cross a barline. The last figure is drawn in 4/2.

Ties

A tie is one sound written as two notes. ~ sits between them, glued or with spaces: /q~/q or C4q ~ C4q. Play the first note and hold it for both. Do not strike the second. The two notes are the same written pitch. Every slash matches every slash. A tie may cross a barline, and the next bar does not reprint that accidental. A chord ties each pitch the next chord shares. A rest or a different pitch stays text. A ~ with no note after it hangs a stub so you can see it while editing. Symbols inserts ~.

Chord symbols on slashes

^ before a slash, note, rest, or chord puts that name above it. A later ^ moves the name. / means play anything: a slash with no stem, one beat. One name over a bar is ^G / / / /. Two names in a bar split the slashes. /q is a rhythm slash, with a stem. % fills the rest of the bar with the repeat sign.

Dynamics and articulation

pp p mp mf f ff sfz sit under the next note. cresc and dim draw a hairpin from that note through the note that carries the next dynamic, or through the last note. On the note itself: ' staccato, > accent, - tenuto, and a bare ^ for marcato (C4e'>, /q^). A ^ followed by text sits above that symbol (/q^P). A ^ at the start of a word is a chord symbol.

Drums

Drums is an exercise kind. The line is kit codes, not pitches. A drum exercise draws the percussion clef and no key signature. Rhythms stays slashes. bd is the bass drum. ft is the floor tom. sd is the snare. mt is the mid tom. ht is the high tom. hh is the hi-hat. hho is the open hi-hat. rd is the ride. rb is the ride bell. cr is the crash. hf is the hi-hat with the foot. Duration letters are the ones you already use. A stack is two sounds at once. feet starts the foot rhythm, and both sides fill the same bars. flam, drag, rim, and xstick sit in front of the hit and take no time. A pitch (C4q), @key=, @treble, @bass, and rs stay text. rs is still a sixteenth rest. Rim is the word rim.

In an exercise

Paste one example per line. Scales, Left Hand Rootless Voicings, Essential Progressions, Play/Rest, and Rhythms are written this way. A Drums exercise holds only kit lines, and Bars and Setup meter work the same way. Keep every line in one exercise the same number of bars, and set Bars to that number. A 6/8 line belongs in an exercise whose Setup meter is 6/8. A line that looks like music but does not parse stays text — an unclosed tuplet or brace, a barline inside a tuplet, a nested tuplet, a tie from a rest, a tie onto a different pitch, a dangling dynamic, or a hairpin with nothing after it. A tuplet of one note draws that note plain. A tie with nothing after it hangs a stub. Drums uses the kit codes above.
