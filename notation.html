How to write on the staff

Some exercises are large text. A line that parses as music is a staff instead. One example per line in Edit. The text below is the line. The staff under it is what you see.

The time signature is not in the line. It follows Setup. These figures use 4/4 unless the figure says otherwise. A line that does not parse stays text.

One pitch, then the durations

Letter, octave. C4 is middle C, a quarter note. After the octave: w whole, h half, q quarter, e eighth, s sixteenth. A dot follows the duration: q.

Clef and key

@treble or @bass, and @key=, in either order. The default is treble, key of C. @key=Eb is E-flat major. @key=Am prints the signature of the relative major (C). A ! after the octave prints the accidental even when the key already has it (F#4!).

A scale

^Major is the name above the staff, one token, before the notes. _1 is the degree under that note. _b3 prints as ♭3. Braces beam eighths and sixteenths into one group.

A chord

Brackets stack a chord. The same duration on every pitch is the clear way to write it. _ after the closing bracket is the chord symbol. The symbol can use ∆.

Rhythm

A slash has no pitch: /q, /e, /q.. Rests are r, rq, rh, rw, re, rs. | is a barline. A caption after · (space, middle dot, space) sits under the staff. _R and _L label a slash.

Beams

Braces force one beam. In 6/8, eighths without braces still group in threes, because the beat is a dotted quarter. Set that meter in Setup. This last figure is drawn in 6/8.

In an exercise

Paste one example per line. Scales, Left Hand Rootless Voicings, and Rhythms are written this way. Keep every line in one exercise the same number of bars, and set Bars to that number. A 6/8 line belongs in an exercise whose Setup meter is 6/8.
