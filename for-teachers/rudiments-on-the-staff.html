For teachers / Rudiments on the staff

A Flam. Then a Paradiddle.

Three rudiments, one example each. Rhythms stays the slash exercise.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 28 Oct 2026 · 4 min read

A page of rudiments looks like a dare. The student starts at the top, plays the one that feels like singles, and the flam is still waiting when the timer goes off. Put the flam on its own line. Then the drag. Then the paradiddle. The click does not care which one they like.

One rudiment. Then the next. The book can stay on the stand.

Snare only, and the bar twice

Open Edit and choose Drums. Each of these examples is two bars of the same bar, so set Bars to 2. There are no feet. flam and drag sit in front of the hit and take no time. _R and _L are the hands. The codes are on How to write on the staff.

Those three are already in Rudiments. Next there is the singles, the doubles, the left-hand lead, and the flam and drag on the paradiddle. You do not paste the rest of the exercise onto this page. Play. Next. The one they skip still comes up.

This did not replace Rhythms

Rhythms stays slashes. A horn player, a pianist, and a drummer who is counting can share that exercise. Rudiments are kit lines in a drum exercise. Snare, sticking, flam, drag. If the assignment is “play this rhythm on anything,” open Rhythms. If the assignment is the snare, open Rudiments.

One dynamic, then the next, is the same idea on slashes: One Dynamic. Then the Next.
