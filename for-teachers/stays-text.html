Three Things the Box Will Not Turn into Kit Notes.

A pitch, a key signature, and rs. Each one fails as a kit note for one reason. Then the line that works.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 28 Oct 2026 · 4 min read

The first drum line a teacher pastes is usually a note they already know how to write. C, a quarter, maybe a key of F, because the horns are in F. The box leaves it as text. That feels like a bug. It is the percussion clef refusing to become a piano.

Three tokens. Three one-line reasons. Then the note you meant.

What stays text

Choose Drums. These three do not become kit notes. The full list of codes is on How to write on the staff.

  • C4q stays text. A pitch is not a piece of the kit.
  • @key=, and @treble and @bass with it, stay text. A drum exercise draws the percussion clef and no key signature.
  • rs is still a sixteenth rest. It is not a ride. A ride sixteenth is rds.

A lone rs can draw, and what it draws is that rest. If you typed it hoping for the cymbal, the staff did not misread you. The rest is the old spelling, and it kept its job.

The three repairs

The ride you wanted instead of C is rdq. The hat and the snare need no signature in front of them. The ride sixteenth is rds, instrument then duration.

If the line stays large text, read the token you hoped was clever. Fix that one word. The staff appears when the line is kit codes. One dynamic that does parse, on a slash exercise, is the neighbor: One Dynamic. Then the Next.
