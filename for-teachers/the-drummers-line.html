For teachers / The drummer's line

The Horns Get a Staff. The Drummer Gets the Kit.

Same chart, two exercises. Section name, one bar, then the simile. The drummer's line is kit codes.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 28 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

Rehearsal starts and the drummer is still looking at the horn parts, counting rests that are not theirs. You say “play time on the intro, then the head.” They play time on everything. Give the horns a pitched exercise. Give the drummer a drum exercise of the same shape. Intro is one example. Head is the next. The click is the rehearsal.

Two exercises. One chart. The drummer stops reading the trumpet.

The horns, then the kit

The horn line is a staff exercise, not Drums. Two bars, so set Bars to 2. ^Intro is the section above the notes. % fills the second bar with the repeat sign. Write the pitches for the chart you are actually teaching. This one is only the shape.

The drummer's exercise is Drums, also Bars 2. Same section name, same simile. The hats and the snare are the groove. The kick is on the foot side. Intro, then Head.

Those two are the start of Chart marks. Next there is Verse, Chorus, and the rest of the section names, including one example at 96. The codes, including ^ and %, are on How to write on the staff.

Save as new on Chart marks when the section names should match the horns. Change ^Intro to the word your chart uses. One word, no spaces: an underscore prints as a space (^B_section). Send two links. The horns Add the pitched exercise. The drummer Adds the kit exercise. Play. Next. This is the lesson plan. It is not a new control on the page.

One exercise for the whole room, when they really are playing the same thing, is One Exercise for the Band Room.
