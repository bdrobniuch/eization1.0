Five and Seven, Counted on the Click.

Two examples, two exercises. You count the clicks. BPM stays the speed of the bottom number.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 28 Oct 2026 · 4 min read

Odd meter lessons love a speech. Five is three plus two, seven is a folk dance, and by the time the speech ends the student has not played a bar. Write one example in 5/4. Write one example in 7/8. They cannot share an exercise, because the meter is on the exercise. Then let the click do the counting.

Five clicks. Then seven clicks. The lecture can wait in the hallway.

Five quarters

Open Edit, choose Drums, set the meter to 5/4, and set Bars to 2. The bottom number is 4, so the BPM number is quarter notes per minute. Five clicks fill the bar. This example groups them 3+2: snare on 3 and 5, kick with that same shape. The codes are on How to write on the staff.

That is the first example of Five four. Next in that exercise is 2+3. Count 1 2 3, 1 2 with the clicks you already have. You do not retune the metronome to make five feel like four.

Seven eighths

New exercise. Drums, meter 7/8, Bars 2. The bottom number is 8, so the same BPM field is eighth notes per minute. A BPM of 120 in 7/8 is 120 eighths. Seven clicks fill the bar. This one groups them 2+2+3, and the beams already show it.

That is the first example of Seven eight. Next in that exercise is 3+2+2. Count the clicks in the groups the beams already drew. Send the two links. Say which number is in the BPM field, and which meter that number belongs to.

6/8 gets its own exercise for the same reason. On the kit, that queue is Six eight, and the bar that holds it twice is Twelve eight. The slash version of “a new meter gets its own exercise” is in A Voicing and a Groove, One Line Each.
