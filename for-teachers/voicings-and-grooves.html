A Voicing and a Groove, One Line Each

One rootless voicing per line. One groove per line. Two exercises, two queues.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 10 Oct 2026 · 7 min read

A ii–V–I written as one blob gets practiced as the I. The ii is the wind-up. The V is a gesture on the way to the chord they like. Split them. A voicing is one example. A groove is another exercise. The click does not let the favorite eat the hour.

One chord, bass clef, parent key

Rootless left-hand voicings live in bass clef. Brackets stack the pitches. h is a half note, and every pitch in the stack should carry it. _Dm7 after the closing bracket is the chord symbol. @key= is the parent key of the tune, here C, so the thirds and sevenths are not a pile of accidentals on the noteheads.

Paste a ii–V–I as three lines. Set Bars to 1. Setup stays in 4/4. Play the voicing on beat 1 and hold it for the bar.

The student who only ever lands on C∆7 still meets Dm7, alone, with the metronome. Add the next pair of roots as new lines when these three are in the hand. The stock Left Hand Rootless Voicings exercise is the same shape across more qualities. Use it as the catalog. Save as new for the three chords from this week’s tune.

∆ in C∆7 is the major-seventh mark. If you would rather type Cmaj7, the staff still prints the symbol you wrote.

A second exercise for the groove

Do not put the rhythm on the same line as the voicing. Time and pitch take turns. Make a second exercise. A slash is an attack with no pitch. A rest is the hole. | is the barline. The name after · sits under the staff. _R and _L are sticking, or any two limbs you name in the lesson.

These three lines are two bars each. Set Bars to 2. Leave Setup in 4/4.

Every line in that exercise should be two bars. A one-bar groove padded into two bars is easier than one Bars number trying to serve two lengths. The stock Rhythms exercise is a longer queue of the same kind of line.

6/8 gets its own exercise

The time signature follows Setup. It is not in the line. A 6/8 groove in a 4/4 exercise will be beamed and clicked in 4/4. Put the compound groove in a new exercise, switch Setup to 6/8, and set Bars to 2. Eighth notes group in threes because the beat is a dotted quarter. Braces do the same thing when you want the group written out.

Two links, one message

Share the voicing exercise and the groove exercise as two links. Write both BPMs in the message. The pianist taps the groove with their hands, then plays the voicings on the next pass. A drummer, or anyone counting, can use the groove exercise alone. Each example comes up once. Then the order changes.
