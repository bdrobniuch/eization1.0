The Lesson Page That Starts the Metronome

The lesson already lived on her page. Practice lived somewhere else, until the exercise sat under the paragraph.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 17 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

She keeps each week’s lesson on her own page. The tune. What to listen for. The lines to practice. Students read it. Then practice lived somewhere else: a link, an app to open, a question about adding the exercise. A lot of them finished the paragraph and never pressed Play.

The page was clear. The next step was a doorway. Some students followed it. Some meant to. The ones who were already late for the next thing closed the tab with the lesson still unread in their hands, which is to say read, and unplayed.

The page still explains the lesson. Under it, one example is already on the screen, and the click is waiting.

Embed, then paste

She writes the lines in eization, the way she would write them for one student. She presses Embed. She pastes the frame under the paragraph. The page still explains the lesson. The student does not leave the page to begin.

The frame is embed=1. There is no intro. There is no question about adding the exercise. The metronome stays stopped until they press Play. Share is still there, for a student who wants that exercise on their own device.

The frame can wear the studio

The mark and the colors can be the studio’s. Wednesday Room below is a made-up studio: a W, wine for play, copper for rest. The click stays stopped until they press Play. On a lesson page, the frame looks like theirs.

That is a link for your studio. Write with the name, the mark, the colors, and the list.

The page you already have is enough. The exercise does not need a second tab. If the whole studio should meet on one exercise before anyone opens a piece, that is the day the room played the same thing.
