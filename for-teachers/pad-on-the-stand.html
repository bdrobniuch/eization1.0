The Pad on the Stand. Not a Sampler.

The phone shows the kit and the click. The drums stay in the room. You play them.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 28 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

A drummer's phone already knows a hundred beats. It will play them, loop them, and let the student air-drum along until the pad practice was a concert they attended. Put the phone on the stand for a different job. One written example, large enough to read from the throne. A click. Next brings the next example. Nothing on that screen hits the drums for them.

The click keeps time. The student plays the kit. The phone does not own a snare.

Two examples, so Next has somewhere to go

Open Backbeat. It is already Drums, already two bars. The first example is the kick on one and three. The next example is the kick on all four. Same hands. The phone has a real Next.

Any other kit exercise on More, under Drums, can be the one on the stand. Pick one. Do not build a playlist of all of them for Tuesday.

If you are writing a line in Edit, the Drums pad is one gesture: tap the piece, then the staff. The codes, if you would rather type, are on How to write on the staff.

The stand rules, stolen

  • Open the share link. Add or Edit the exercise.
  • Phone on the stand. Notifications off. If it buzzes, you restart the example.
  • One example. Play. Next brings the next kick, not a video of someone else's fill.

The practice page asks to keep the display on while that page is in front. If the device refuses, a muted loop stands in. Switching apps still lets the phone sleep. The longer version of the stand, for any instrument, is They Will Doomscroll. Put the Phone on the Stand.

The groove and the fill as two exercises, when the pad is at home and the lesson is the writing, is One Groove on the Staff. Then the Next.
