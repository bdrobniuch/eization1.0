For teachers / Phone on the stand

They Will Doomscroll for 40 Minutes. They Will Not Practice Bar 2.

This is not a lecture about kids these days. It is a studio hack for the device that already won.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 21 Aug 2026 · 7 min read

Your student can find a forty-minute rabbit hole about shoe reviews, drum fills, and a stranger arguing with a stranger about a band neither of you teach. Ask them to stay with bar 2 of an etude and the room suddenly needs water, a snack, a different reed, and a short philosophical inquiry into whether practice “counts” if it is not filmed.

This is not laziness in the moral sense. It is an interface mismatch. The feed is designed to deliver the next thing. Practice is often designed as a wall of paper and a metronome that looks like it was borrowed from 1987.

Do not fight the phone. Put one clean example on it, with a click, and make Next feel like the next useful scroll.

The ban that never survives the hallway

Plenty of good teachers ban phones in the lesson. Fair. Then the student goes home, where the phone is the desk lamp, the tuner, the metronome app with more skins than the student has scales, the recording device, and the social life. The ban does not travel.

AirPods in a rehearsal still make me want to invent a new facial expression. That does not mean the device is useless for practice. It means the default use is wrong.

What “practiced” means on a glowing rectangle

When practice lives as a PDF, a photo of your handwriting, or a voice note that says “do the hard part,” the student still chooses the order. Comfortable bars win. The feed wins between bars.

Change the job of the screen. One example. Large enough to read from the stand. Metronome running. When that example is done, Next. Each example in the exercise comes up once. Then the order changes. The dopamine of “what’s next” stays—but the next thing is the next musical example, not a comment section.

Keep the screen awake while they play

A phone that goes dark mid-phrase is a tiny tragedy and a perfect excuse to unlock and “just check.” On the practice page, eization asks to keep the display on while that page is in front: screen wake lock when the device allows it, and a muted looping fallback when it does not (older iOS, some Home Screen installs, Low Power Mode). Switching apps still lets the device sleep. Low Power Mode can still win. Be honest with students about that.

Put the phone on the stand. Landscape if it helps. Start Play. Teach them that unlocking for anything else ends the set—same rule as leaving the room during a take.

A script you can steal

  • “Open the share link I sent. Add the exercise.”
  • “Phone on the stand. Notifications off. If it buzzes, we restart the example.”
  • “One example. Play. When I say Next—or when the bars fill—you get the next one.”
  • “You do not pick the order. That is the whole point.”

What this is not

This is not an argument that apps replace teachers, or that attention spans are doomed forever, or that paper is evil. Paper is great. Teachers are the point. The phone already lives in the practice room. You can let it be a slot machine, or you can make it a stand for one example under a click.

Buying another metronome skin will not make Tuesday’s lesson go better. Changing what appears when they tap Next might.
