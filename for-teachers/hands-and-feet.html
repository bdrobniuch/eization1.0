Hands and Feet on One Line.

Hat eighths over kick quarters need feet. A stack is two sounds that share one duration.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 28 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

A drummer can play hat eighths and a kick on one and three without thinking. The moment you ask them to write it, the line becomes a paragraph. They try to put the kick inside the hats, the bar comes out the wrong length, and the staff stays text. The word that fixes it is already in the line. It is feet.

A stack shares one duration. Feet is how two rhythms share one bar.

Hats in eighths, kick in quarters

Open Edit and choose Drums. This example is one bar, so set Bars to 1. Eight hat eighths fill the bar. The kick and the rests fill that same bar in quarters. feet starts the foot rhythm. Both sides have to fill the same bars, or the line stays text. The codes are on How to write on the staff.

Hands stem up. The kick stems down. You did not write two staves. You wrote one line with two jobs.

When they share the column

A stack is one duration. Kick and snare in the same brackets happen together, for that one length. Here each column is a quarter: hat with snare, then hat with kick.

You cannot stack the hat eighths with the kick quarters. They are not the same length. That is what feet is for. When the snare lands on a hat eighth, the stack is the right tool again, and the kick stays on the foot side.

The queues that already use this

The kick moving under those hats is Backbeat. The foot hat on 2 and 4 is Hat openings. Open one of those when the week is the queue. These three lines are the explanation. They are not a fourth exercise to memorize.

One groove at a time, including the fill, is One Groove on the Staff. Then the Next.
