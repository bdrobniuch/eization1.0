Practice That Fits in a Carry-On

Hotel carpet, quiet hours, twelve minutes. One example on the phone, with the metronome.

By Błażej Drobniuch · 4 Oct 2026 · 7 min read

Travel practice is not a character flaw. It is a constraint problem. The room is small. The neighbors have opinions. The instrument may be a mute, a pad, or nothing but fingers on a table. The clock offers twelve minutes before the lobby call—or forty minutes of airport carpet that will not wait for your perfect warm-up ritual.

This is different from the doomscroll problem. That story is about attention stealing the hour. This one is about place: you would practice if the setup fit the room you are actually in.

Pack the exercise before you leave. Do not invent the queue at gate C47.

What to pack (it is software, mostly)

Before you go: open eization and make sure the exercise you need is on the phone that will travel. Stock exercises work. A custom list you built at home works. A share link from a teacher works—Add it before you lose wifi. Teachers assigning travel homework can follow Homework That Survives Thursday.

Keep the list short. A road exercise is not your life’s work. Six to ten examples. Bars matched to what you can actually play quietly. Tempo written where you will see it.

Phone as stand, click as witness

Put the phone where you can read one example without leaning into a laptop. Play keeps time. Next brings the next one. Each example comes up once; then the order changes. That matters more on the road than at home: you will not “just start with the easy key” for forty minutes because the queue will not cooperate.

While the practice page is in front, eization asks to keep the display on: wake lock when the device allows it, and a muted looping fallback when it does not (older iOS, some Home Screen installs, Low Power Mode). Switching apps still lets the device sleep. Low Power Mode can still win. Be honest with yourself about that—airplane mode and a charged battery beat magical thinking.

Queues that fit twelve minutes

  • One scale shape, few keys—not the whole atlas.
  • Four rhythm cells you can clap or tap.
  • Six short lines from the book chapter you are living with.
  • Tone prompts: long / soft attack / subito piano—one per line.

If you teach touring students, send them a share link sized for travel, not for a three-hour shed session. Coverage under a short clock still counts.

What this will not fix

eization will not make the hotel walls thicker. It will not replace a practice mute you forgot. It will not be a band in your headphones. Its job on the road is narrow: one example present, time keeping, and a queue that does not collapse into Measure 3 because you are tired.
